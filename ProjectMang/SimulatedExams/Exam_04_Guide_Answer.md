### Section B: Critical Statement Analysis & Conceptual Discrimination (10 Marks)
*Grading Scheme: Award 0.5 Mark for correct True/False classification, and 1.5 Marks for a complete, theoretically sound justification or error correction.*

#### Statement 1: "Strategic corporate objectives are directly prioritized and governed by individual Project Managers."
- **Official Verdict:** **FALSE** [0.5 Mark]
- **Full Mark Rationale & Correction [1.5 Marks]:**
  Strategic corporate objectives are selected, prioritized, and governed through Portfolio Management. Project Management focuses on tactical goals to deliver specific deliverables.

#### Statement 2: "The Closing Process Group is the most frequently neglected and abandoned phase in project management."
- **Official Verdict:** **TRUE** [0.5 Mark]
- **Full Mark Rationale & Correction [1.5 Marks]:**
  Closing is statistically the most frequently neglected process group because teams are eager to disband and move on to new initiatives without formal contract closure or lessons learned archiving.

#### Statement 3: "The Project Charter is formally created, approved, and issued by the Project Manager."
- **Official Verdict:** **FALSE** [0.5 Mark]
- **Full Mark Rationale & Correction [1.5 Marks]:**
  The Project Charter is formally issued by an entity external to the project, such as an executive sponsor or PMO leadership. The charter formally grants authority *to* the Project Manager.

#### Statement 4: "A project inherently involves substantial uncertainty regardless of how well it is initially planned."
- **Official Verdict:** **TRUE** [0.5 Mark]
- **Full Mark Rationale & Correction [1.5 Marks]:**
  Because every project is a temporary endeavor creating a unique deliverable, it inherently involves uncertainty that is progressively elaborated over time.

#### Statement 5: "Top management commitment is statistically identified as the number one factor associated with IT project success."
- **Official Verdict:** **TRUE** [0.5 Mark]
- **Full Mark Rationale & Correction [1.5 Marks]:**
  Top management commitment is the primary success factor required to secure adequate resources, remove bureaucratic barriers, and mandate inter-departmental cooperation.

### Section D: Deep Conceptual Explanations & Essay Problems (10 Marks)

#### Question 1: The Four Organizational Frames and Stakeholder Navigation (5 Marks)
**Marking Scheme & Model Answer:**

1. **Structural Frame (1.25 Marks):** Focuses on roles, organizational charts, formal responsibilities, and reporting lines. *Application:* Re-evaluates job descriptions, establishes clear governance committees, and defines RACI charts to clarify responsibilities.
2. **Human Resources Frame (1.25 Marks):** Focuses on aligning organizational demands with personal employee needs, skills, and job satisfaction. *Application:* Provides comprehensive training programs, reduces workload anxiety, and ensures employees feel valued rather than threatened by automation.
3. **Political Frame (1.25 Marks):** Views organizations as coalitions of interest groups competing for scarce power, influence, and resources. *Application:* Identifies influential informal leaders, negotiates with department heads who fear losing territory/authority, and builds coalitions of project champions.
4. **Symbolic Frame (1.25 Marks):** Focuses on corporate culture, symbols, rituals, traditions, and the meaning of events. *Application:* Creates launch ceremonies, rebrands the initiative positively, aligns with corporate legacy, and establishes new cultural traditions of excellence.

#### Question 2: Organizational Culture Profiles and Project Success Determinants (5 Marks)
**Marking Scheme & Model Answer:**

1. **Definition of Organizational Culture (1.5 Marks):** A set of shared assumptions, values, behaviors, norms, and artifacts that characterize the functioning of an organization and govern how employees interact and perform work.
2. **Five Favorable Cultural Dimensions (2.5 Marks - 0.5 each):**
   - **High Member Identity:** Employees identify with the overall company rather than just their departmental silo.
   - **Group / Team Emphasis:** Work is organized around teams rather than isolated individuals.
   - **High Risk Tolerance:** Innovation and calculated experimentation are encouraged, treating honest mistakes as learning opportunities.
   - **High Conflict Tolerance:** Open discussion and constructive confrontation of differences are embraced rather than suppressed.
   - **Open-Systems Focus:** The organization actively monitors and responds to external environmental and market changes.
3. **Impact of Unit Integration (1 Mark):** Strong unit integration means departments cooperate, communicate seamlessly, and share common objectives. In such environments, functional managers readily loan skilled staff and share information, eliminating departmental turf wars and making the PM's integration and coordination duties significantly smoother.

### Section F: Real-World Applied Case Study & Problem Solving (10 Marks)

#### Scenario Analysis: Enterprise ERP Implementation: Turnaround from Total Collapse

**Comprehensive Model Solution & Evaluation Criteria:**

1. **Process Groups Remediation (3 Marks):**
   - *Root Cause (1.5 Marks):* Skipping pre-initiation meant no financial return (ROI) or strategic feasibility was proven. Skipping Initiating left the project without an authorized scope baseline, budget authority, or identified stakeholder expectations.
   - *Mandatory Documents (1.5 Marks):* A formal **Business Case** (justifying financial investment and cost-benefit analysis) and a formal **Project Charter** (signed by the CEO/Executive Sponsor authorizing scope, budget, and PM authority).
2. **Operations vs. Projects Conflict (3 Marks):**
   - *Inherent Conflict (1.5 Marks):* Operations are ongoing, routine, and urgent (e.g., getting trucks dispatched today); projects are temporary and unique. When employees juggle both, immediate operational fires always starve project activities of time and energy.
   - *Structure Impact (1.5 Marks):* In functional/weak matrix structures, functional managers evaluate employee bonuses based on daily operations, ensuring team members neglect project tasks.
3. **Top Management Commitment & PMO (4 Marks):**
   - *Top Management Role (2 Marks):* Top management commitment is the #1 project success factor. The executive sponsor must publicly mandate the ERP as the company's top strategic priority, mandate functional manager cooperation, and secure dedicated funding.
   - *PMO Role (2 Marks):* A PMO establishes standardized project management methodologies, institutes disciplined change control boards (CCB) to stop rogue custom modifications, manages enterprise resource capacity, and monitors baseline health.
