#### Question 10 [Chapter 3]
**Correct Answer:** **a) Initiate, plan, control, execute, close** [1 Mark]

> **Detailed Justification:** The correct answer is "Initiate, plan, control, execute, close". These five process groups represent the sequential stages of a project's life cycle. The initiate phase involves identifying and defining the project's objectives and stakeholders. The plan phase involves creating a detailed project plan, including tasks, resources, and timelines. The control phase involves monitoring and managing the project's progress and making necessary adjustments. The execute phase involves carrying out the project tasks according to the plan. The close phase involves completing and delivering the project, as well as conducting a final review and documenting lessons learned.

### Section B: Critical Statement Analysis & Conceptual Discrimination (10 Marks)
*Grading Scheme: Award 0.5 Mark for correct True/False classification, and 1.5 Marks for a complete, theoretically sound justification or error correction.*

#### Statement 1: "Good project management practices directly result in a lower cost of capital for the organization."
- **Official Verdict:** **FALSE** [0.5 Mark]
- **Full Mark Rationale & Correction [1.5 Marks]:**
  Good project management improves worker morale, shortens development schedules, and increases profit margins, but does NOT lower an organization's cost of capital (interest rates / cost of borrowing equity).

#### Statement 2: "A milestone list is formally categorized as an output of Project Scope Management."
- **Official Verdict:** **FALSE** [0.5 Mark]
- **Full Mark Rationale & Correction [1.5 Marks]:**
  A milestone list is an output of Project Schedule (Time) Management, produced specifically during the 'Define Activities' process.

#### Statement 3: "Strong unit integration within an organizational culture makes the project manager's job significantly more difficult."
- **Official Verdict:** **FALSE** [0.5 Mark]
- **Full Mark Rationale & Correction [1.5 Marks]:**
  Strong unit integration means departments collaborate and coordinate smoothly across boundaries, which makes the project manager's job significantly easier.

#### Statement 4: "Dress codes, workplace rituals, and meeting etiquette are classified under the Human Resources organizational frame."
- **Official Verdict:** **FALSE** [0.5 Mark]
- **Full Mark Rationale & Correction [1.5 Marks]:**
  Dress codes, rituals, traditions, and workplace atmosphere belong to the Symbolic Frame (culture and meaning). The Human Resources frame focuses on personal employee needs, salaries, and staffing policies.

#### Statement 5: "Strategic corporate objectives are directly prioritized and governed by individual Project Managers."
- **Official Verdict:** **FALSE** [0.5 Mark]
- **Full Mark Rationale & Correction [1.5 Marks]:**
  Strategic corporate objectives are selected, prioritized, and governed through Portfolio Management. Project Management focuses on tactical goals to deliver specific deliverables.

### Section D: Deep Conceptual Explanations & Essay Problems (10 Marks)

#### Question 1: Project vs. Program vs. Portfolio Management Hierarchy (5 Marks)
**Marking Scheme & Model Answer:**

1. **Project Management (Tactical) (1.5 Marks):**
   - **Focus:** Tactical execution of individual projects.
   - **Objective:** Direct application of knowledge, tools, and skills to activities to meet specific project requirements, deliver unique outputs, and honor the triple constraint.
2. **Program Management (Coordinated Tactical/Strategic) (1.5 Marks):**
   - **Focus:** Group of related projects managed in a coordinated way.
   - **Objective:** Harvest collective benefits and control not available from managing them individually (e.g., economies of scale, unified architecture, coordinated release).
3. **Portfolio Management (Strategic) (1.5 Marks):**
   - **Focus:** Enterprise-wide collection of projects, programs, and other operational work.
   - **Objective:** Strategic selection, prioritization, governance, and resource allocation to ensure investments directly align with corporate business goals and maximize ROI.
4. **Strategic vs. Tactical Distinction (0.5 Marks):** Tactical goals answer *'Are we carrying out projects the right way?'* (within scope, time, budget). Strategic goals answer *'Are we investing in the right projects?'* (maximizing organizational value).

#### Question 2: Subprojects and Risk Granularity in Project Management (5 Marks)
**Marking Scheme & Model Answer:**

1. **Definition of Subproject (2 Marks):** A subproject is a manageable, distinct division or component of an overall larger project, frequently treated as an independent project with its own defined scope, team, schedule, and budget, while contributing directly to the parent project's objectives.
2. **Three Operational Advantages (3 Marks - 1 Mark each):**
   - **Granular Control & Monitoring:** Dividing an intricate enterprise system into subprojects allows specialized project managers to track milestones, tasks, and budgets at a higher resolution, preventing bottlenecks from being obscured.
   - **Risk Isolation & Mitigation:** Technical failures, supply chain delays, or regulatory hurdles can be contained within a specific subproject without automatically halting the entire master project. Specialized risk response strategies can be deployed targetedly.
   - **Specialized Resource & Team Efficiency:** Subprojects allow assigning cross-functional teams with niche technical proficiencies (e.g., database architecture vs. mobile frontend vs. compliance) to operate autonomously without excessive cross-team communication overhead.

### Section E: Analytical Comparison & Trade-Off Matrix (5 Marks)

#### Solution Matrix: Hierarchy of Management: Project vs. Program vs. Portfolio Management (5 Marks)

| Comparison Criterion | Project Management | Program Management | Portfolio Management |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Strategic vs. Tactical** | Strictly Tactical | Tactical and Operational Coordination | Strictly Strategic |
| **Scope & Boundaries** | Specific, narrowly defined deliverables and unique outputs | Broad scope encompassing multiple interrelated projects | Enterprise-wide organizational business scope |
| **Change Approach** | Strict change control to preserve baselines | Manages changes across component projects to harvest synergy | Continuously adapts investments to corporate strategic shifts |
| **Success Metric** | Triple constraint delivery (Scope, Time, Cost) & quality | Realization of aggregate business benefits and collective ROI | Aggregate enterprise return, risk balance, and strategy alignment |
| **Leadership Focus** | Task execution, team guidance, and technical coordination | Program synergy, inter-project dependency, stakeholder alignment | Executive governance, capital allocation, and business value |

*Grading Rubric (5 Marks):* Award 1 Mark per correctly contrasted criterion. Critical trap: Project Management is tactical; Portfolio Management is strategic.
