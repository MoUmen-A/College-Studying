#### Statement 1: "The Project Charter is formally created, approved, and issued by the Project Manager."
- **Official Verdict:** **FALSE** [0.5 Mark]
- **Full Mark Rationale & Correction [1.5 Marks]:**
  The Project Charter is formally issued by an entity external to the project, such as an executive sponsor or PMO leadership. The charter formally grants authority *to* the Project Manager.

#### Statement 2: "A project inherently involves substantial uncertainty regardless of how well it is initially planned."
- **Official Verdict:** **TRUE** [0.5 Mark]
- **Full Mark Rationale & Correction [1.5 Marks]:**
  Because every project is a temporary endeavor creating a unique deliverable, it inherently involves uncertainty that is progressively elaborated over time.

#### Statement 3: "Top management commitment is statistically identified as the number one factor associated with IT project success."
- **Official Verdict:** **TRUE** [0.5 Mark]
- **Full Mark Rationale & Correction [1.5 Marks]:**
  Top management commitment is the primary success factor required to secure adequate resources, remove bureaucratic barriers, and mandate inter-departmental cooperation.

#### Statement 4: "Schedules are statistically verified as the single most common cause of conflict throughout a project's life cycle."
- **Official Verdict:** **TRUE** [0.5 Mark]
- **Full Mark Rationale & Correction [1.5 Marks]:**
  Statistical research across global projects identifies project schedules as the most frequent and intense source of conflict among stakeholders.

- **Official Verdict:** **FALSE** [0.5 Mark]

  Subprojects reduce overall risk by decomposing complex endeavors into manageable, isolated segments, allowing localized risk mitigation and granular progress tracking.

### Section D: Deep Conceptual Explanations & Essay Problems (10 Marks)

#### Question 1: Project Life Cycle vs. Product Life Cycle (SDLC) & Phase Exits (5 Marks)
**Marking Scheme & Model Answer:**

1. **Project Life Cycle vs. Product Life Cycle (2 Marks):**
   - **Project Life Cycle:** A collection of project phases (typically Concept, Development/Design, Implementation, Close-out) applying to all projects regardless of domain. It has a strict beginning and end.
   - **Product Life Cycle (SDLC):** Spans the entire multi-year existence of a product from initial inception, through development, operations, maintenance, enhancements, and eventual decommissioning. A product life cycle often encompasses dozens of individual projects.
2. **Phase Exits / Kill Points (1.5 Marks):** Management reviews held at the conclusion of each project phase to evaluate progress, verify deliverables, and decide whether to continue, redirect, or terminate the project. They prevent organizations from pouring good money after bad into flawed initiatives.
3. **Cost of Changes Across Life Cycle (1.5 Marks):** In early phases (concept/feasibility), changes require updating documents or drawings, costing very little. In late phases (implementation/close-out), changes necessitate scrapping written code, redesigning hardware, re-testing, and disrupting operations, making changes exponentially more expensive.

#### Question 2: Agile Scrum vs. Predictive Process Groups Integration (5 Marks)
**Marking Scheme & Model Answer:**

1. **Agile Process Groups Mapping (2 Marks):**
   - **Initiating:** Vision statement creation, initial Product Backlog definition, and Product Owner appointment.
   - **Planning:** High-level release planning followed by iterative, granular Sprint Planning meetings where the team pulls user stories into the Sprint Backlog.
   - **Executing:** Self-organizing development team building deliverable working software increments during 2-4 week Sprints, coordinated via Daily Scrums.
   - **Monitoring & Controlling:** Burndown charts, Daily Scrums, and Sprint Reviews demonstrating working software to stakeholders.
   - **Closing:** Sprint Retrospectives analyzing process improvements, deliverable deployment, and backlog reprioritization.
2. **Roles and Artifacts (3 Marks - 1 Mark each):**
   - **Product Owner:** Represents business value and stakeholders; owns and prioritizes the Product Backlog, ensuring the highest-value features are planned first.
   - **ScrumMaster:** Servant leader who facilitates ceremonies, eliminates impediments, shields the team from external distractions, and ensures agile practices are upheld.
   - **Sprint Backlog:** The definitive output of Sprint Planning; represents the selected user stories and technical tasks committed for completion during the current execution cycle.

### Section F: Real-World Applied Case Study & Problem Solving (10 Marks)

#### Scenario Analysis: Cybersecurity Infrastructure Defense & Scope Recovery

**Comprehensive Model Solution & Evaluation Criteria:**

1. **Scope Creep & Change Control (4 Marks):**
   - *Definitions (1.5 Marks):* **Scope Creep** is the unauthorized, uncontrolled expansion of product or project scope without adjustments to time, cost, and resources. **Progressive Elaboration** is the planned, continuous refinement of existing, agreed-upon scope as detailed requirements emerge.
   - *Change Control Process (2.5 Marks):* 
     1. Submit a formal written Change Request.
     2. PM performs impact analysis assessing effects on Scope, Time, Cost, and Quality.
     3. Submit analysis to the Change Control Board (CCB) and Sponsor for formal approval/rejection.
     4. If approved, formally revise the Project Management Plan baselines and communicate to all stakeholders.
2. **Knowledge Area Authority (3 Marks):**
   - *Knowledge Area (1 Mark):* **Project Integration Management**, which coordinates and unifies processes across all knowledge areas.
   - *Asserting Control (2 Marks):* The PM must issue an immediate stop-work order on unauthorized biometric coding, audit expended hours, explain contractual protocol to the technical team, and enforce that only approved WBS activities receive resource allocation.
3. **Baselines & Stakeholders (3 Marks):**
   - *Baselines Rule (1.5 Marks):* Baselines represent the formal approved plan. Unilateral modifications destroy performance tracking, distort Earned Value, and expose the vendor to unpaid work.
   - *Stakeholder Action (1.5 Marks):* Update the Stakeholder Register to classify the Chief Risk Officer as a high-power, high-influence stakeholder. Meet with him to present the change request cost/schedule impact, requiring formal bank budget approval for added features.
