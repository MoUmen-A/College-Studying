### Section B: Critical Statement Analysis & Conceptual Discrimination (10 Marks)
*Grading Scheme: Award 0.5 Mark for correct True/False classification, and 1.5 Marks for a complete, theoretically sound justification or error correction.*

#### Statement 1: "Project scope changes are significantly more expensive to implement during early project phases than during execution."
- **Official Verdict:** **FALSE** [0.5 Mark]
- **Full Mark Rationale & Correction [1.5 Marks]:**
  Changes made during early phases (concept and design) are very inexpensive; as the project progresses into execution and testing, changes become exponentially costlier due to rework and disrupted deliverables.

#### Statement 2: "The Planning process group consumes the greatest percentage of project time and financial resources."
- **Official Verdict:** **FALSE** [0.5 Mark]
- **Full Mark Rationale & Correction [1.5 Marks]:**
  Executing consumes the vast majority of project time, effort, and financial budget (>50%). Planning contains the highest *number* of individual processes (>20), but not the highest resource consumption.

#### Statement 3: "Good project management practices directly result in a lower cost of capital for the organization."
- **Official Verdict:** **FALSE** [0.5 Mark]
- **Full Mark Rationale & Correction [1.5 Marks]:**
  Good project management improves worker morale, shortens development schedules, and increases profit margins, but does NOT lower an organization's cost of capital (interest rates / cost of borrowing equity).

#### Statement 4: "A milestone list is formally categorized as an output of Project Scope Management."
- **Official Verdict:** **FALSE** [0.5 Mark]
- **Full Mark Rationale & Correction [1.5 Marks]:**
  A milestone list is an output of Project Schedule (Time) Management, produced specifically during the 'Define Activities' process.

#### Statement 5: "Strong unit integration within an organizational culture makes the project manager's job significantly more difficult."
- **Official Verdict:** **FALSE** [0.5 Mark]
- **Full Mark Rationale & Correction [1.5 Marks]:**
  Strong unit integration means departments collaborate and coordinate smoothly across boundaries, which makes the project manager's job significantly easier.

### Section D: Deep Conceptual Explanations & Essay Problems (10 Marks)

#### Question 1: Triple Constraint Dynamics & Progressive Elaboration (5 Marks)
**Marking Scheme & Model Answer:**

1. **Triple Constraint Definition (2 Marks):** The triple constraint represents the balancing act between three competing primary project goals: **Scope** (what work will be done to produce the unique product/service/result), **Time** (the project schedule and deadlines), and **Cost** (the approved budget and financial resources). Quality is often positioned at the center as a quadrupled constraint affected by all three.
2. **Interaction & Trade-Offs (1.5 Marks):** Changing any one constraint inevitably impacts at least one other constraint. For example:
   - Expanding Scope requires increasing either Time (lengthening schedule) or Cost (adding more staff/budget), or risking Quality degradation.
   - Compressing Time (crashing/fast-tracking) increases Cost or necessitates reducing Scope.
   - Slashing Cost requires cutting Scope or extending Time due to fewer allocated resources.
3. **Progressive Elaboration & Uncertainty (1.5 Marks):** Progressive elaboration means continuously developing in detail and clarifying project parameters as more information becomes available. Early in the project, uncertainty is highest and estimates have wide variance; as planning progresses through iterations, the scope and constraints become clearly defined, steadily decreasing uncertainty towards project completion.

#### Question 2: PMBOK Process Groups Architecture & Process Interrelationships (5 Marks)
**Marking Scheme & Model Answer:**

1. **The Five Process Groups (2.5 Marks):**
   - **Initiating:** Formally defines and authorizes a project or phase; identifies initial scope, budget, and key stakeholders (Key outputs: Business Case, Project Charter, Stakeholder Register).
   - **Planning:** Devises a workable scheme to accomplish project goals; encompasses the highest number of individual processes (>20) to establish scope, schedule, and cost baselines.
   - **Executing:** Coordinates personnel and resources to carry out the project management plan and build deliverables; consumes the vast majority of time, effort, and budget (>50%).
   - **Monitoring & Controlling:** Regularly measures actual performance against baselines, identifies variances, and manages change requests via Integrated Change Control.
   - **Closing:** Formalizes stakeholder acceptance, closes contracts (*Close Procurement*), archives records, and captures lessons learned (most frequently neglected group).
2. **Process Groups vs. Project Phases (1.5 Marks):**
   - Process Groups are collections of interrelated processes grouped by outcome, not chronological project phases.
   - Phases are chronological divisions (e.g., Feasibility, Design, Prototype, Production). In a multi-phase project, **all five process groups are typically executed within each distinct phase**.
3. **Dynamic Overlap (1 Mark):** Process groups do not occur in isolated sequential 'stop-and-start' fashion. They overlap continuously. For example, while Executing is ongoing, Monitoring & Controlling runs concurrently, and feedback regularly triggers progressive elaboration in Planning.

### Section E: Analytical Comparison & Trade-Off Matrix (5 Marks)

#### Solution Matrix: Organizational Structures: Functional vs. Matrix (Weak/Strong) vs. Projectized (5 Marks)

| Evaluation Dimension | Functional Organization | Weak Matrix | Strong Matrix | Projectized Organization |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **PM's Formal Authority** | Little or none | Low (minimal authority) | Moderate to High | High to Almost Absolute |
| **Resource Availability** | Controlled entirely by Functional Manager | Controlled by Functional Manager | Shared, PM has strong negotiating power | Controlled entirely by Project Manager |
| **Budget Control** | Functional Manager | Functional Manager | Project Manager | Project Manager |
| **Primary Role of PM** | Part-time / Expeditor / Assistant | Project Coordinator / Expeditor | Full-time Project Manager | Full-time Project Manager / Program Lead |
| **Team Commitment** | Loyal exclusively to functional department | Split loyalty, leans heavily toward functional dept | Split loyalty, balanced or leans toward project | Loyal exclusively to the project team |

*Grading Rubric (5 Marks):* Award 1 Mark for each row accurately completed. Emphasize that in a Weak Matrix, the PM is merely a coordinator with no true authority, whereas in Projectized structures, the PM commands the budget and dedicated resources.

### Section F: Real-World Applied Case Study & Problem Solving (10 Marks)

#### Scenario Analysis: Hospital Electronic Health Records (EHR) Modernization Crisis

**Comprehensive Model Solution & Evaluation Criteria:**

1. **Organizational & Frame Diagnosis (3 Marks):**
   - *Structure Impediment (1 Mark):* In a Functional organization, the PM has virtually no formal authority over personnel. Department heads control resources and prioritize departmental routine over project needs.
   - *Political Frame Resolution (1 Mark):* The PM must build coalitions with influential physician leaders, establish a medical advisory steering committee, and negotiate protected clinical release time with functional heads.
   - *Symbolic Frame Resolution (1 Mark):* The PM must reshape the narrative from 'administrative burden' to 'patient safety and saving lives,' creating adoption ceremonies, celebrating early milestones, and respecting medical traditions.
2. **Governance & Initiating (3 Marks):**
   - *Consequence (1 Mark):* Proceeding without a charter left the PM without executive backing, formal authority, or agreed boundaries, inviting chaos and departmental defiance.
   - *Issuer (1 Mark):* The Project Charter must be formally issued by an executive *external* to the project, such as the Hospital CEO or Board of Directors.
   - *Authority Conferred (1 Mark):* Formally authorizes project existence and empowers Dr. Bennett to command organizational resources, enforce participation, and spend approved funds.
3. **Triple Constraint Trade-Off (4 Marks):**
   - *Analysis (2 Marks):* With Time strictly fixed by regulation, attempting to deliver 100% scope with unbudged cost will inevitably crash **Quality**, causing medical errors.
   - *Recommended Trade-Off (2 Marks):* The PM should employ an agile phased rollout: strictly reduce initial **Scope** to core regulatory mandates (Minimum Viable Product), temporarily increase **Cost** (hiring temporary clinical scribes/support staff to ease physician burdens during rounds), thereby safeguarding **Quality** and clinical safety.
