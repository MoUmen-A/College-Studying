# Chapter 2: The Project Management and Information Technology Context

## Executive Summary

Chapter 2 examines the organizational environment and context within which projects operate, applying systems thinking, organizational theory, life cycle models, and modern IT trends:
1. **The Systems Approach:**
   - **Systems Philosophy:** An overarching model for viewing things as interconnected systems.
   - **Systems Analysis:** A structured problem-solving approach that defines system scope, decomposes it into components, and evaluates issues, opportunities, constraints, and requirements.
   - **Systems Management:** Addressing the business, technological, and organizational dimensions of creating, operating, and modifying systems.
   - **Three-Sphere Model of Systems Management:** Projects must harmonize three distinct domains: **Business**, **Organization**, and **Technology**.
2. **The Four Organizational Frames:**
   - **Structural Frame:** Focuses on roles, responsibilities, hierarchy, formal reporting, and organizational charts.
   - **Human Resources Frame:** Strives to produce harmony between organizational demands and human/employee needs.
   - **Political Frame:** Views the organization as a coalition of varied individuals and interest groups competing for scarce power and resources.
   - **Symbolic Frame:** Focuses on symbols, meaning, corporate culture, dress codes, work ethic, rituals, and the significance of events.
3. **Organizational Structures:**
   - **Functional Structure:** Staff grouped by specialization (engineering, finance, IT); PM has minimal or no formal authority.
   - **Matrix Structure (Weak, Balanced, Strong):** Staff report to dual superiors (functional manager and PM). In a *Weak Matrix*, the PM acts merely as a coordinator or expeditor.
   - **Project (Projectized) Structure:** PM holds direct, comprehensive authority over personnel and resources.
4. **Organizational Culture:** Project success is maximized in cultures featuring strong unit integration, high member identity, group/team emphasis, high risk tolerance, and balanced control.
5. **Life Cycle Frameworks:**
   - **Project Life Cycle:** Concept $\rightarrow$ Development $\rightarrow$ Implementation $\rightarrow$ Close-out. (Phase exits or kill points occur after each phase to determine continuation).
   - **Product Life Cycle (SDLC):** Spans the entire life of a product through operations and retirement; uses Predictive (Waterfall), Prototyping, Iterative/Spiral, or Adaptive (ASD, Agile/Scrum) models.
6. **Key IT Trends:** Globalization, Offshoring/Outsourcing, Virtual Teams, and Agile Project Management.

## Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

> [!WARNING]
> Pay close attention to these frequently tested nuances to avoid losing marks:

1. **Three-Sphere Model Distractor:**
   - *Trap:* Selecting "Information" as part of the Three-Sphere Model.
   - *Rule:* The three spheres are strictly **Business**, **Organization**, and **Technology**. "Information" is incorrect.
2. **Symbolic Frame vs. Human Resources Frame:**
   - *Trap:* Attributing dress codes, meeting norms, and work hours to the Human Resources frame.
   - *Rule:* Dress codes, meeting etiquette, traditions, and workplace atmosphere belong to the **Symbolic Frame** (culture and symbols). Human Resources deals with meeting personal needs, salaries, and staffing policies.
3. **Systems Analysis vs. Systems Management Definitions:**
   - *Trap:* Confusing systems analysis with systems management.
   - *Rule:* **Systems analysis** is the problem-solving technique of decomposing a system into component parts. **Systems management** is addressing the business, technological, and organizational issues of a system.
4. **Weak Matrix Project Manager Authority:**
   - *Trap:* Assuming a project manager in a weak matrix has formal authority.
   - *Rule:* In a weak matrix, the PM is merely a **coordinator and expeditor** with zero to minimal authority; functional managers retain authority.
5. **Timing and Cost of Project Changes:**
   - *Trap:* Answering "True" to the statement that changes are more expensive during early phases.
   - *Rule:* Changes during early phases (concept/feasibility) are **very inexpensive**; changes become **exponentially costlier** during execution and close-out.
6. **Stakeholder Influence Timeline:**
   - *Trap:* Believing stakeholder influence increases as the project nears completion.
   - *Rule:* Stakeholder influence is **highest at the start** of the project and steadily **declines** as decisions become locked in and deliverables are finalized.
7. **Unit Integration Impact on PM:**
   - *Trap:* Believing that strong unit integration makes the PM's job more difficult.
   - *Rule:* Strong unit integration means departments collaborate and communicate smoothly, making the PM's job **significantly easier**.
8. **Origin of PM Standards and Guidelines:**
   - *Trap:* Agreeing that project management standards "must be derived by top management".
   - *Rule:* Top management **supports, funds, and enforces** standards, but standards are actually **developed and crafted** by project managers and the PMO.
9. **Top Management vs. Functional Management:**
   - *Trap:* Thinking functional management commitment is sufficient to secure adequate resources.
   - *Rule:* **Top management commitment** is the number one success factor required to secure funding, cross-departmental cooperation, and project visibility.
10. **Internal vs. External Stakeholder Classification:**
    - *Trap:* Classifying government regulators, neighboring communities, or citizens as internal stakeholders.
    - *Rule:* Government bodies, regulators, customers, suppliers, and citizens are **external stakeholders**.

## Part 4: Short Answer & Essay Questions

### 1. Why is there a new or renewed interest in the field of project management?
**Answer:**  
More and more projects are being done by a variety of organizations. The projects are more complex and often involve the use of new technologies. Organizations are struggling to find better ways to manage their projects.

---

### 2. What does it mean to take a system view of a project? How does taking a systems view of a project apply to project management?
**Answer:**  
Taking a system view means looking at the big picture of how a particular project fits into the rest of the organization. It is important for project managers to understand the broader organizational environment to ensure their projects meet organizational needs.

---

### 3. Explain the four frames of organizations. How can they help project managers understand the organizational context for their projects?
**Answer:**  
The four frames of organizations are summarized below:
- **Structural:** Deals with how the organization is structured and focuses on roles and responsibilities. It’s important to understand these roles and responsibilities when dealing with project stakeholders, especially in procuring resources.
- **Human Resources:** Focuses on meeting the needs of the organization and its people. Project managers must understand various human resources policies and procedures.
- **Political:** Addresses organizational and personal politics. Many project managers fail because they do not understand the political environment.
- **Symbolic:** Focuses on symbols and meanings. It’s important to understand an organization’s culture, dress code, work ethic, and rituals in managing projects.

---

### 4. Briefly explain the differences between functional, matrix, and project organizations. Describe how each structure affects the management of the project.
**Answer:**  
- **Functional organizations:** Have managers or vice presidents in specialties such as engineering, manufacturing, information technology, and human resources. Staff members have specialized skills in their respective disciplines. Project managers have minimal or no authority.
- **Project organizations:** Have project managers reporting directly to the CEO, with full authority over resources and project budgets. Personnel are dedicated exclusively to project work.
- **Matrix organizations:** Represent the middle ground between functional and project structures. Personnel report to both a functional manager and one or more project managers. Structures can be weak, balanced, or strong matrix.
- **Authority comparison:** Project managers have the most authority in project organizational structures, followed by matrix, and least in functional structures.

---

### 5. Describe how organizational culture is related to project management. What type of culture promotes a strong project environment?
**Answer:**  
Organizational culture is a set of shared assumptions, values, and behaviors that characterize the functioning of an organization. This culture directly impacts project management. For example, if an organization values project management and follows guidelines for applying it, practicing good project management will be much easier.  
Project work is most successful in an organizational culture where:
- Employees identify more with the organization as a whole (high member identity).
- Work activities emphasize groups and teams rather than individuals.

### 7. What are the phases in a traditional project life cycle? How does a project life cycle differ from a product life cycle? Why does a project manager need to understand both?
**Answer:**  
- **Traditional Project Life Cycle Phases:** Concept, development, implementation, and close-out. These phases are common to all projects.
- **Difference from Product Life Cycle:** Product life cycles vary tremendously based on the nature of the product/system (e.g., SDLC models such as waterfall, spiral, prototyping, incremental, or RAD). A product life cycle spans the initial idea through operations and retirement, often encompassing multiple projects (e.g., feasibility study project, design project, implementation project, training project).
- **Need to Understand Both:** Project managers must understand both to integrate project deliverables into the long-term operational success of the product.

### 8. What makes IT projects different from other types of projects? How should project managers adjust to these differences?
**Answer:**  
IT projects are different from other projects because they can be very diverse in terms of size and complexity, they often involve team members with very diverse backgrounds and skills, and the underlying technologies evolve rapidly.  
Project managers should adjust to these differences by paying careful attention to project goals and actively communicating to align the needs of various technical and business stakeholders.

### 9. Define globalization, outsourcing, virtual teams, and agile project management, and describe how these trends are changing IT project management.
**Answer:**  
- **Globalization:** Has created a connected world where communication occurs across borders and the playing field is leveled for international participants.
- **Outsourcing:** When an organization acquires goods and/or services from an outside source.
- **Virtual Teams:** A group of individuals who work across time, space, and organizational boundaries using communication technologies.
- **Agile Project Management:** A method for managing projects with iterative development, short cycles, and high customer collaboration when requirements are unclear or rapidly evolving.
- **Impact on Management:** These trends make co-located project teams rare, increasing the complexity of coordination, communication, cultural alignment, and management across time zones.
