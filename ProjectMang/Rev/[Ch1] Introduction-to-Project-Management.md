# Chapter 1: Introduction to Project Management

---

## Executive Summary

Chapter 1 introduces the core principles, terminology, and foundational framework of project management:
1. **Definition of a Project:** A temporary endeavor undertaken to create a unique product, service, or result. It has a defined beginning and end, unique purpose, requires resources from various areas, involves uncertainty, and is progressively elaborated.
2. **Operations vs. Projects:** Operations are ongoing and repetitive work designed to sustain business; projects are temporary and unique initiatives designed to achieve specific goals.
3. **The Triple Constraint:** The delicate balance between **Scope**, **Time**, and **Cost** goals. Quality is often considered a central quadrupled dimension.
4. **Hierarchy of Management:**
   - **Project Management (Tactical):** Direct application of knowledge, tools, and skills to individual project activities to meet specific project requirements.
   - **Program Management:** Managing a group of related projects in a coordinated manner to harvest collective benefits not available from managing them individually.
   - **Portfolio Management (Strategic):** Centralized selection, prioritization, and governance of all enterprise projects, programs, and related work to achieve corporate strategic business objectives.
5. **Project Manager Role & Skills:** Responsible for accomplishing project goals through stakeholder communication (the most vital trait), leadership by example, organizational prowess, and understanding project technology.
6. **Key Organizations & Standards:** Project Management Institute (**PMI**), Project Management Professional (**PMP**) credential, and the Project Management Office (**PMO**).

## Common Mistakes & Exam Traps to Avoid

> [!WARNING]
> Pay close attention to these frequently tested nuances to avoid losing marks:

1. **Strategic vs. Tactical Misattribution:**
   - *Trap:* Stating that Project Management addresses strategic goals.
   - *Rule:* **Portfolio Management** addresses **strategic** goals; **Project Management** addresses **tactical** goals.
2. **Operations vs. Projects Characteristics:**
   - *Trap:* Marking "ongoing and repetitive" or "routine tasks" as project features.
   - *Rule:* Projects are strictly **temporary** and **unique**. Routine, ongoing tasks are **operations**.
3. **Degree of Uncertainty:**
   - *Trap:* Believing that well-planned projects involve "little uncertainty".
   - *Rule:* Projects inherently involve **substantial uncertainty** due to their unique deliverables and progressive elaboration.
4. **Advantages of Good Project Management:**
   - *Trap:* Selecting "lower cost of capital" as an advantage of project management.
   - *Rule:* Good PM improves morale, speeds up development time, and yields higher profit margins, but does **not** lower the organizational cost of capital.
5. **Who Issues the Project Charter:**
   - *Trap:* Assuming the project manager issues the charter.
   - *Rule:* The project charter is formally issued by an entity **external to the project** (the sponsor or initiating executive) at an appropriate authority level. The charter grants authority to the PM.
6. **PM Responsibilities vs. Technical Expertise:**
   - *Trap:* Assuming a certified PMP is responsible for ensuring technical engineering specifications are correct.
   - *Rule:* The PM manages project integration, constraints, and stakeholder communication; technical experts validate technical accuracy.
7. **Initiative Alignment Order:**
   - *Trap:* Creating a WBS or procuring servers immediately when assigned a new business transformation project.
   - *Rule:* Always align on **vision, scope, and mission** first through a working session and **tailor existing organizational process assets (OPAs)** before defining detailed scope or building the WBS.

## Part 3: Short Answer & Essay Questions

### 1. Why is there a new or renewed interest in the field of project management?
**Answer:**  
More and more projects are being done by a variety of organizations. The projects are more complex and often involve the use of new technologies. Organizations are struggling to find better ways to manage their projects.

---

### 2. What is a project, and what are its main attributes? How is a project different from what most people do in their day-to-day jobs? What is the triple constraint? What other factors affect a project?
**Answer:**  
- **Definition:** A project is *"a temporary endeavor undertaken to create a unique product, service or result."*  
- **Main Attributes of a Project:**
  - Unique purpose
  - Temporary
  - Require resources, often from various areas
  - Should have a primary sponsor and/or customer
  - Involve uncertainty
- **Differences from Day-to-Day Operations:** Projects differ from day-to-day operational activities primarily because they have focused goals and definite beginning and ending dates, whereas operations are repetitive and continuous.  
- **The Triple Constraint:** Managing scope, time, and cost goals.  
- **Other Factors Affecting Projects:** Quality, risk, human resources, communications, and stakeholders.

---

### 3. What is project management? Briefly describe the project management framework, providing examples of stakeholders, knowledge areas, tools and techniques, and project success factors.
**Answer:**  
- **Definition:** Project Management is the application of knowledge, skills, tools, and techniques to project activities to meet project requirements.  
- **Project Management Framework:** The framework graphically shows the process of beginning with stakeholders’ needs and expectations, applying the project management knowledge areas and various tools and techniques to lead to project success and then enterprise success.  
- **Example:** If a project were to implement an ERP system for a large company:
  - **Stakeholders:** Managers and users from many different departments (finance, manufacturing, human resources, IT, etc.).
  - **Knowledge Areas:** All knowledge areas would be important (integration, scope, time, cost, quality, HR, communication, risk, procurement, stakeholder).
  - **Tools & Techniques:** Gantt charts, work breakdown structures, project charters, cost estimations, status reports.
  - **Success Factors:** Implementing key functions by a certain time for a certain cost, and having the new system pay for itself within a specified time period.

---

### 4. What is a program? What is a project portfolio? Discuss the relationship between projects, programs, and portfolio management and the contributions that each makes to enterprise success.
**Answer:**  
- **Program:** A group of related projects managed in a coordinated way.  
- **Project Portfolio:** A collection of projects or programs and other work that are grouped together to facilitate effective management of that work to meet strategic business objectives.  
- **Relationship:** Projects are part of programs, which are part of portfolios.  
- **Enterprise Contributions:** 
  - Individual projects deliver tactical outputs.
  - Programs deliver coordinated benefits and synergy across related projects.
  - Portfolio management ensures that the organization invests in the right projects and programs that directly support corporate strategy and maximize return on investment.

### 5. What is the role of the project manager? What are suggested skills for all project managers and for IT project managers? Why is leadership so important for project managers? How is the job market for IT project managers?
**Answer:**  
- **Role:** The Project Manager is the person responsible for accomplishing the project objectives.  
- **Suggested Skills:** Strong leadership skills, organizational skills, technical skills, and many soft skills (communication, negotiation, conflict resolution).  
- **Skills for IT Project Managers:** IT project managers require the same general project management skills, but they should also understand the specific technology used for the project and understand the motivations and work styles of people who work on IT projects.  
- **Importance of Leadership:** Leading by example is the most important trait of effective project managers; inspiring and guiding teams through uncertainty is vital to reaching project goals.  
- **Job Market:** The job market for information technology project managers continues to remain strong, especially for those with strong business and leadership skills.

### 7. What is the Difference Between Project and Program?
**Answer:**  
- A **project** is a one-time venture having specific objectives and deliverables within a defined timeline.
- A **program** is a set of related projects that share a larger objective, often spanning longer durations and involving coordinated efforts across multiple projects and teams.
- The program vs. project objectives, lifecycle, and practical examples can be used to distinguish them clearly.

### Key Summary Notes
- **Scope & Duration:** The main difference between a project and a program is the scope and duration; projects are typically singular tasks with a definitive time frame while programs are ongoing activities with a less defined end date.
- **Career Relevance:** Understanding the differences between these two concepts can be useful for individuals wanting to work in project or program management.
- **Distinct Roles:** Both project and program management require specific roles; project managers are responsible for the setup, plan, and implementation of projects, whereas program managers oversee the organization's programs and their success.
